== divination | the-theory ==
Divination is the art of reading the unknown — the hidden, the distant, the yet to
come — from a sign. Cicero split it two ways, and the split still holds: natural
divination (dreams, visions, prophecy, direct inspiration) and artificial
divination (a method worked on a sign — cards, lots, figures, the flight of birds,
the stars). The methods share one hidden assumption: that the moment and its chance
marks are not empty, but mirror the whole — "as above, so below." Whether one reads
that as the cosmos answering, as the mind casting itself on a rich symbol-set, or as
pattern found in noise, the machinery below is the same.

== natural-and-artificial ==
The two engines of all divination. Natural divination needs no apparatus: the sign
comes unbidden — a dream, a vision, a prophet's word, a sudden knowing. Artificial
divination builds a sign on purpose and reads it by rule: you shuffle, cast, count
or observe, then interpret by a fixed code. Most systems in this book are
artificial — their worth is in the discipline of the method, which forces a
scattered question into a shaped answer. The doctrine of signatures (that a thing's
look declares its virtue and its kin) is the same instinct turned on nature.

== geomancy | the-sixteen-figures ==
Geomancy ("earth-divination") is a figure-casting art out of the medieval Arabic
world (ʿilm al-raml, "the science of the sand"), carried into Latin Europe by the
12th century and hugely popular to the 17th. The method: make four rows of random
marks — dots pricked in sand or on paper, fast and unthinking — and tally each row
odd or even to get one or two dots. Four such rows build one figure of four levels;
do it four times for four "Mother" figures. From the Mothers the rest of the chart
is generated by fixed rule.

== geomancy-method | mothers-to-judge ==
From the four Mothers, geomancy derives the whole reading by mechanism, not fresh
casting. The four Daughters are made by reading the Mothers in columns instead of
rows. Mothers and Daughters, paired and added (odd+odd=even, and so on, level by
level), give four Nieces; the Nieces give two Witnesses (Right and Left); the two
Witnesses give the Judge, one figure that answers the question, tested for
soundness against the First Mother. The sixteen figures are often also cast into
the twelve astrological houses. A wholly deterministic engine turning on one
scatter of dots.

== the-figures | the-sixteen ==
The sixteen geomantic figures, each four levels of one or two dots, each with a
name, an element, a planet and a zodiac sign, read as good, ill or mixed. The
chief ones: Via (the Way) and Populus (the People); Fortuna Major and Fortuna
Minor (the greater and lesser good fortune); Acquisitio (gain) and Amissio (loss);
Laetitia (joy) and Tristitia (sorrow); Puella (the girl) and Puer (the boy);
Albus (white, peace) and Rubeus (red, passion); Conjunctio (union) and Carcer
(the prison); Caput and Cauda Draconis (the Dragon's Head and Tail, threshold in
and out). Each is read by its own nature and by the house it falls in.

== i-ching | the-book-of-changes ==
The I Ching (Yijing), China's Book of Changes, is among the oldest divinatory and
philosophical books alive, its core of the Zhou dynasty (c. 1000 BC). It rests on
one stroke doubled: the yang line (unbroken) and the yin line (broken), the active
and the yielding. Stack three lines and you have one of eight trigrams; stack two
trigrams and you have one of the 64 hexagrams, each a named situation with a text
of judgement and image, and commentaries (the "Ten Wings") layered on since. Less
a fortune-teller than a mirror of change itself.

== the-eight-trigrams | bagua ==
The eight trigrams (bagua), each three lines of yin and yang, are the alphabet of
the I Ching and of much Chinese thought: Qian (☰, Heaven, the creative), Kun (☷,
Earth, the receptive), Zhen (☳, Thunder, the arousing), Kan (☵, Water, the
abysmal), Gen (☶, Mountain, stillness), Xun (☴, Wind/Wood, the gentle), Li (☲,
Fire, the clinging), Dui (☱, Lake, the joyous). They stand for family roles,
directions, elements and forces, and arranged in a ring (the Early and Later
Heaven orders) they ground feng shui as well as the oracle.

== i-ching-casting | changing-lines ==
How a hexagram is got. The old way divides 50 yarrow stalks through three
handlings per line, six times, bottom to top; the quick way throws three coins per
line, heads and tails tallied. Each line comes out yin or yang, and some come out
"old" (changing) — a yang about to become yin, or the reverse. You read the
hexagram first as cast; then, if any lines are changing, you read their special
texts and change them to get a second hexagram — the situation and what it is
becoming. Change built into the method is the whole point.

== runes | elder-futhark ==
The runes are the old Germanic alphabets, the Elder Futhark (c. 150-800 AD) being
the eldest, 24 letters named for things — Fehu (cattle, wealth), Uruz (the aurochs,
strength), Thurisaz (the thorn/giant), Ansuz (a god, Odin, the mouth), on through
Dagaz (day) and Othala (the homestead) — grouped in three "aettir" of eight. They
were a working script for carving on stone, wood and bone. Honesty demands a flag:
that runes were cast as a divinatory oracle is attested only thinly in antiquity
(Tacitus describes a lot-casting with marked wood), and the modern practice of
"rune reading" is largely a 20th-century revival — Ralph Blum's 1982 book invented
much of it, the blank "Wyrd" rune included.

== numerology | gematria | isopsephy ==
Number-divination reads meaning in the numbers behind names and words. Where a
script uses its letters as numerals — Hebrew (gematria), Greek (isopsephy) — every
word has a sum, and words of equal sum are held secretly akin, a tool of Kabbalah
and of scriptural reading (and the root of "666," a name counted). Pythagoras gave
the West the notion that number is the ground of things and that each number 1-9
has a character. Modern "numerology" reduces a birth-date or name to a single digit
and reads it — a late, popular simplification of that old and deeper number-
mysticism.

== scrying | the-speculum ==
Scrying is seeing in a speculum: a crystal ball, a black mirror, a bowl of water or
ink, a polished surface, a flame. The seer softens the gaze until shapes, letters
or scenes arise, read as message. It needs no fixed code, so it sits on the
"natural" side — the sign is what the seeing mind throws up. From the pythia's
vapours to Dr Dee's shewstone, it is the common engine of vision-divination; the
discipline is of attention, stilled and patient, more than of apparatus.

== augury-and-haruspicy | omens ==
Rome read the will of heaven in the world itself. The augur watched the sky — the
flight, cries and feeding of birds, the lightning, the quarter of the heavens they
came from — to take the auspices before any public act. The haruspex (an Etruscan
art) read the entrails of a sacrifice, above all the liver, its lobes mapped like a
little sky. Both are omen-reading: the sign is unsummoned and public, the method a
fixed lore of what each portent means. The ancestor of reading tea-leaves, salt and
the black cat alike.

== sortilege | cleromancy | bibliomancy ==
Divination by lots (sortes) is the simplest art of all: cast or draw, and let the
fall decide — dice, knucklebones, marked sticks, drawn straws, the Urim and Thummim
of the Hebrew priests, the sortes of Roman shrines. Bibliomancy is its bookish
form: open a revered book (Homer, Virgil, the Bible, the Quran) at random and read
the first line struck as the answer (the sortes Virgilianae). The assumption is
bare and pure — that chance, in the charged moment, is not chance. Flipping a coin
on a hard choice is its last honest descendant.

== horary-astrology | the-question-chart ==
Horary astrology divines not from a birth but from a question: a chart is cast for
the exact moment and place the question is asked and understood, and read for the
answer. The querent and the quesited are given their houses and planets (the
"significators"), and the answer is judged by how those planets aspect and move —
do they come to meet (yes) or separate (no), helped or hindered on the way. Rigid
rules guard it (the "considerations before judgement" warn when a chart is unfit to
read). The most method-bound of the astral divinations, and the closest to the
diviner's craft.

== dream-divination | oneiromancy ==
Reading dreams for omen and meaning is among the oldest divinations, squarely on
the "natural" side — the sign comes unbidden in sleep. The ancients sorted dreams
(Macrobius) into the meaningful and the meaningless, and kept dream-books keying
images to outcomes (Artemidorus's Oneirocritica, 2nd c., is the great one). The
tradition holds some dreams god-sent or soul-sent, others mere churnings of the
day's residue and the body's state — a distinction the modern study of dreams, by
other means, still roughly keeps.
