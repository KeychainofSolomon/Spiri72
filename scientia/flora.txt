== barley | hordeum | bere ==
Barley (Hordeum vulgare), a grass sown for grain. Cold and dry in the first
degree by the old reckoning. A water of boiled barley (ptisan) was given to
cool fevers and ease the chest and the bladder; the whole grain, parched and
ground, thickened pottage and small-beer. Steeped and sprouted, it is malt, the
ground of ale. Of little virtue against cold humours, being itself cold.

== betony | betonica | stachys | bishopwort ==
Betony (Betonica officinalis). Hot and dry in the second degree. Long held a
master-wort against head-ache, and against frightful dreams and the falling of
the mind; carried as a ward against ill spirits. The dried leaf was taken as a
powder, or steeped as a tea, for the head and the stomach.

== rue | ruta | herb-of-grace ==
Rue (Ruta graveolens). Hot and dry in the third degree. Bitter and strong; a
little sharpens the sight and was reckoned to quicken the wit and cool unchaste
heat. Taken too freely it burns and harms, and it is forbidden to women with
child. Set near other plants it was thought to guard them.

== mandrake | mandragora ==
Mandrake (Mandragora officinarum). Cold in the third degree, and narcotic. The
forked root was likened to a man and hedged with tales of its scream when drawn.
Used in small measure as a sleep-bringer and against pain before the knife; in
any excess a poison that stupefies. Handle as a strong simple, never as a food.

== vervain | verbena | holy-herb ==
Vervain (Verbena officinalis). Temperate, leaning hot and dry. A herb of
cleansing and of oaths, strewn and carried for protection and used in washes for
sores of the mouth and skin. More esteemed in rite and ward than in the pot.

== fennel | foeniculum | marathron ==
Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare). Hot and dry in the second degree. Seed and root
open stoppages, ease wind in the belly, and were chewed to stay hunger on fast
days; a water of it was dropped to clear the eyes. Mild and kindly in use.

== wormwood | artemisia | absinthium ==
Wormwood (Artemisia absinthium). Hot in the first degree, dry in the third, and
bitter above most herbs. It strengthens a weak stomach, stirs the appetite, and
was steeped in wine against worms and low spirits. Bitter enough that a little
serves; too much offends the head.

== mugwort | artemisia-vulgaris | motherwort ==
Mugwort (Artemisia vulgaris). Hot and dry. A traveller's herb, laid in the shoe
against weariness, and a woman's herb for the courses. Hung in the house and
burned as a smoke it was held to drive off what is unclean, and to bring on
clear and telling dreams.
