== solomonic-magic | clavicula | key-of-solomon ==
The Solomonic art is the Western grimoire tradition of binding spirits by divine
authority, laid under the name of King Solomon, who in legend commanded the
spirits with a God-given ring and seal. Its root book is the Clavicula Salomonis
(Key of Solomon), a Latin and Italian work of the 16th-17th centuries (older
roots behind it), surviving in many manuscripts and printed by S.L. MacGregor
Mathers in 1889. Its heart is this: the operator works not by his own power but
by the Names of God, in a state of purity, at the right time, with consecrated
tools. Described here as the tradition prescribes it, not as a claim that it
works.

== the-lemegeton | lesser-key ==
The Lemegeton, or Lesser Key of Solomon, is a 17th-century compilation of five
books, each a different order of spirit-work. Ars Goetia: the 72 demons, their
seals and offices, bound by conjuration. Ars Theurgia-Goetia: 31 aerial spirits
of the compass (drawn from Trithemius). Ars Paulina: the angels of the 24 hours
and of the degrees of the zodiac. Ars Almadel: the angels of the four
"altitudes." Ars Notoria: prayers and figures for infused learning — the oldest
part, of the 13th century. Spiri72's own frame descends from the first of these.

== ars-goetia | the-seventy-two ==
The Ars Goetia gives 72 spirits (kings, dukes, princes, marquises, presidents,
earls, knights), each with a name, a rank, a tally of legions, a seal, and
offices — to teach, to reveal, to reconcile or to destroy. The operator calls one
by its seal and office, within the circle, constrains it to the triangle, binds
it to truth and to the task, then licenses it to depart. The brass vessel stands
by for the disobedient. This is the frame the Spiri72 project renders in
software, office and seal and licence alike.

== the-magic-circle ==
The circle is the operator's ground and guard: a ring (nine feet across in the
Goetia) inscribed with divine names and drawn with rite, within which he stands
protected. The spirit, called into the world, cannot cross it. Everything of the
operation is ordered and sealed — nothing left to chance is the point. In the
tradition its power is not the chalk but the Names written in it and the purity
of the one who stands there; the circle is authority made visible.

== the-triangle-of-art ==
Set outside the circle, a little way off, stands the Triangle of Art, bound with
names and often with the divine name around its sides — the one place the spirit
is constrained to appear and be held. The operator never leaves the circle; the
spirit never leaves the triangle. (Spiri72 keeps this exactly: the spirit's seal
is set in the triangle, and there it answers.) A vessel of black mirror or smoke
was sometimes set in it for the spirit to manifest within.

== the-divine-names ==
The engine of the whole art is the names of God, by which — not by the operator's
own will — the spirits are compelled. Tetragrammaton (YHVH), Adonai, Elohim, El,
Ehyeh, Shaddai, and a great many more, Hebrew and Greek and corrupt Latin, are
spoken and written on circle, seal and lamen. The operator conjures "in the name
of" this authority, as a herald bears a king's word. This is the deep principle:
no power is the magician's own — the same law Annulus is built on.

== the-pentacles | planetary-pentacles ==
The Key of Solomon gives 44 pentacles — discs of names and figures, seven planets'
worth — each for a purpose: to open the earth's treasure, to win favour, to turn
back harm, to call or bind spirits of that planet. Made of the planet's metal or
drawn in the right colour, in its day and hour, consecrated. They are the
talismanic side of the art: not tools of force but seals of authority, carried or
shown to command what answers to that planet.

== the-instruments | the-tools ==
The operator's tools, each consecrated with rite and made at the right hour: the
wand or staff (command), the sword and the knives (one black-hilted, one white,
for the circle and for cutting), the lamen or pentacle worn on the breast (the
operator's warrant and guard), the ring (Solomon's, to look through or to cover
the face against a spirit's visage), the censer, the vessel, and clean garments
of linen. The making and keeping of them is half the discipline.

== planetary-hours | the-timing ==
Every work is set to its time. The day and its night each split into twelve
unequal "planetary hours," ruled in turn by the seven planets in the Chaldean
order (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon), the first hour of each
day giving that day its planet. Add the Moon's phase (waxing to build, waning to
loose) and the right sign. A work done in its planet's hour is held to be in
tune with what it calls on. Spiri72 enforces this in its own hours and rules.

== purification | preparation ==
Before any calling, the operator prepares: days of fasting and abstinence,
chastity, confession and prayer, a ritual bath, clean (often new) garments, a
clean and private place. The tools and the book are consecrated. The tradition
holds the operation only as strong as the operator's purity and resolve — an
unready or fearful operator is in danger. Preparation is most of the work; the
calling is brief.

== the-conjuration | the-constraint ==
The calling escalates. First the invocation, naming the spirit, its seal and
office, by the divine names. If it tarries, stronger conjurations; then the
"constraint" and the Spirits' Chain, binding it to come; last the curse or
"spirit of cursing," threatening the brass vessel and the fire until it appears.
Once present, it is bound to truth and set its task. Every step is by the Names
and the seals, never by threat alone — the force is borrowed authority, applied
in measure.

== the-brass-vessel ==
The Brass Vessel is the legendary prison of the 72: Solomon, the tale runs, shut
the spirits in a brass vessel sealed with his seal and cast it into a deep lake,
whence it was later broken open. In the operation it stands by, threatened, as
the last constraint for a spirit that will not obey or depart. (Spiri72 keeps a
"Vessel" of its own, where a banished spirit's refused work is quarantined.)

== licence-to-depart ==
No calling is ended until the spirit is dismissed with the Licence to Depart —
"go in peace to your place, and peace be between us" (vade in pace). To leave a
spirit undismissed is held the real danger of the art, worse than failing to call
it. The dismissal is courteous, not triumphant. Spiri72 enforces this: every
rite must end in a licence, a holding or a banishment, and the ink runs out if
none is given.

== ars-notoria ==
The Ars Notoria, oldest layer of the Lemegeton (13th century), is not spirit-
constraint but angelic and divine prayer-magic for infused knowledge: by set
orations and intricate figures (notae) gazed upon in fasting, the operator sought
to receive memory, eloquence and the mastery of the liberal arts and sciences
from God and his angels in a flash, without study. The ancestor of the dream of
learning poured in, not earned.

== the-heptameron ==
The Heptameron, ascribed to Peter de Abano (really later, 16th c.), is a terse
practical handbook for conjuring the angels and spirits of the seven days and
their planetary hours: the circles, names, seals and conjurations for each day,
the ruling angel, its ministers, the airs and winds. Compact and much-used, it
sits between the angelic and the goetic styles, and lent much to the Lemegeton's
timing and forms.

== how-it-works | the-theory ==
The tradition's own account: the cosmos is a great chain from God down through
angels, stars and elements to earth, all bound by sympathy, so that the higher
rules the lower and like answers to like. Spirits are powers within that order.
The operator commands them not by his own might but as a delegate holding God's
Names and Solomon's warrant, purified so as to be a fit vessel, timing his work to
the stars so it runs with the grain of things. Modern eyes read it as symbol,
psychology or theatre; set down here is how the art understood itself.
