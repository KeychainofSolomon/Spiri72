== angel-magic | theurgy ==
Angel magic, or theurgy ("god-work"), is the brighter twin of the goetic art: in
place of constraining spirits, the operator petitions holy angels with prayer,
purity and the Names of God, seeking knowledge, protection or divine favour, and
is meant to be raised toward God in the doing. Where goetia binds a lower spirit
by authority, theurgy beseeches a higher one by devotion. Same century, same
Names, opposite posture. Described here as the tradition framed it.

== the-nine-choirs | celestial-hierarchy ==
The ranks of heaven, set out by Pseudo-Dionysius (c. 500 AD) and kept through the
Middle Ages, are nine in three triads. First and highest, nearest God: Seraphim,
Cherubim, Thrones. Second: Dominions, Virtues, Powers. Third, nearest the world:
Principalities, Archangels, Angels. Each choir has its office in governing the
cosmos and passing God's light downward. (The 72 goetic spirits are, in the
lore, angels fallen from these orders — which is why some of them still name the
choir they fell from.)

== the-seven-archangels | planetary-angels ==
Seven great angels were matched to the seven planets, most famously by Agrippa:
Zaphkiel to Saturn, Zadkiel to Jupiter, Camael (Chamael) to Mars, Michael to the
Sun, Haniel (Anael) to Venus, Raphael to Mercury, Gabriel to the Moon. (The
assignments shift between sources — Michael and Raphael in particular are set now
to Sun, now to Mercury — so treat the list as one common scheme, not a fixed
law.) Called in their planet's day and hour, they are the angelic rulers of the
heptarchic and talismanic arts.

== the-shemhamphorash | seventy-two-angels ==
The Shem ha-Mephorash, the "divided Name," yields 72 angels — the bright mirror
of the 72 goetic demons. It is drawn from three verses of Exodus (14:19-21), each
of exactly 72 Hebrew letters; read together in a braided order they give 72
three-letter names, and to each is added a divine suffix (-iah or -el) to make an
angel's name. These 72 were mapped to the degrees of the zodiac and the days of
the year. The neat one-to-one pairing of each angel against a Goetia demon,
though, is a late (19th-century, Mathers-era) systematization, not an old one.

== john-dee | enochian | angelic-magic ==
The most storied angel-working of the age is that of Dr John Dee, Elizabeth I's
philosopher, with the scryer Edward Kelley (1581-1587). Through Kelley's gazing in
a crystal "shewstone," they recorded a whole system said to be dictated by
angels: an angelic language with its own letters and grammar, 48 "Keys" or Calls,
and four great elemental Tables or Watchtowers of angelic names. It is primary
and genuine as Dee's record — but the name "Enochian" and much of the ceremonial
order came later, from the Golden Dawn (1888+), who rebuilt the system to their
own ends.

== the-angelic-keys | watchtowers ==
Dee's system centres on the Great Table: four Watchtowers, one per element, each
a grid of letters yielding the names of ruling angels, kings and lesser powers,
and a central "Tablet of Union." The 48 Angelic Keys (Calls) are invocations in
the angelic tongue to open the gates of these tablets and their "Aethyrs" (thirty
heavens). What Dee set down plainly as received is one thing; the elaborate
workings built on it since are another, and worth keeping apart.

== the-sigillum-dei-aemeth ==
The Sigillum Dei Aemeth — the "Seal of God's Truth" — is an intricate disc of
names of God and angels enclosed in a heptagram and heptagon, which Dee made to
Kelley's angelic instructions and set under the shewstone and the holy table. A
far older figure (it appears in medieval magic) that Dee received anew. Its
office was to ground and guard the scrying, as the Solomonic circle guards the
conjuror — the angelic art's answer to the circle.

== trithemius | steganographia ==
Johannes Trithemius (1462-1516), abbot and scholar, wrote the Steganographia
(c. 1499, printed 1606), outwardly a work of angel magic — angels named to carry
messages across great distance — but in good part a veiled cryptography, its
"conjurations" hiding ciphers. He also gave the De Septem Secundeis, the "seven
secondary intelligences," planetary angels ruling the ages of the world in turn.
A hinge figure: angel magic and the birth of secret-writing in one hand.

== ars-almadel ==
The Ars Almadel (fourth book of the Lemegeton) works the angels of the four
"altitudes" or choras — the heights of heaven answering to the seasons and
quarters. The operator makes the almadel itself: a square tablet of coloured wax
set on four wax feet, marked with divine names, with candles, and scries the
angel appearing above it. A gentle, wholly angelic rite, for lawful and worthy
petitions only, the texts insist.

== ars-paulina | angels-of-the-hours ==
The Ars Paulina (third book) governs the angels of time and sky: the ruling
angels of the 24 hours of the day and night, each with its planetary ground, and
the angels of the 360 degrees of the zodiac, tied to the operator's own natal
star. Ascribed, falsely, to the Apostle Paul and his vision of the third heaven.
It weds the angelic art tightly to astrology — the right angel for the right hour
and degree.

== scrying | the-shewstone ==
Scrying is seeing in a speculum — a crystal ball or shewstone, a black mirror, a
bowl of water or ink, a polished nail. The diviner (or a gifted "skryer," as
Kelley was for Dee) gazes until figures, letters or scenes arise, read as shown
by spirit or angel. It is the common thread of angelic revelation: the circle and
Names make the ground safe, the stone is the window. The real workings are of
attention and the seeing mind; the tradition credited the angels.

== how-it-works | theurgy-theory ==
The angelic art's own account: the soul can be raised, by purity, prayer and the
divine Names, to commune with the higher powers and through them with God, and so
receive light, knowledge and grace poured down the same great chain the goetic
art reaches down into. Its safeguards are inward — worthiness, right intent,
devotion — more than the outward bonds of the goetic circle, for one does not
chain an angel but is lifted by it. The peril named is not a raging spirit but
self-deception: a lying spirit in angel's dress, which is why the tradition tests
what it is shown.
